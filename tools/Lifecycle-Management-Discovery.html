Fleet level and instance level are managed separately. Management components (VCF Operations, VCF Automation, fleet management, Operations for logs, Operations for networks, Identity Broker) sit at fleet level. Core components (SDDC Manager, vCenter, NSX Manager, ESX) sit at VCF Instance level. Several checks below only make sense once you know which one you are talking about.

Step 3 of 8

Foundation and depot

Nothing else in lifecycle management works until binaries can be retrieved, the fleet inventory is accurate, and there is an agreed target Bill of Materials. This section is the load bearing wall.

Step 4 of 8

Patching and upgrades

Release types, component order, the plan and precheck workflow, and the reduced downtime methods that make frequent patching realistic. The gap between disclosure and exploitation is now days, so speed of patching is a security control.

Step 5 of 8

Password management

Fleet wide policy, the four distinct credential operations, and automatic rotation. Expired or mismatched credentials are one of the most common precheck blockers, which is why this sits alongside patching rather than off in a security backlog.

Step 6 of 8

Certificate management

Certificate authority configuration, replacement of default deployment certificates, and expiry handling. Bulk operations and automatic renewal are what make this sustainable at fleet scale.
